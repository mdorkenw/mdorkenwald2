Michael Dorkenwald

I’m an AI Scientist Intern at Mistral AI, working on multimodal large language models across pretraining and post-training. I’m also a PhD candidate at the University of Amsterdam / ELLIS, advised by Yuki Asano and Cees Snoek.

My research focuses on vision–language learning, video understanding, and computational efficiency.

Previously, I was a Student Researcher at Google DeepMind and a Research Intern at Amazon Web Services (AWS) on the Rekognition team.

Spatial groundingCVPR 2024First author

PIN: Positional Insert Unlocks Object Localisation Abilities in VLMs

M. Dorkenwald, N. Barazani, C. Snoek, Y. Asano

We introduce PIN, a learned spatial prompt that enables frozen vision–language models to localize objects. Training uses synthetic data and next-token prediction, without manually annotated detection data.

Adaptation of OpenFlamingo and BLIP-2 · Zero-shot object localization

Paper Training code Project
Temporal understandingTVBench · BMVC 2025Equal contribution

Lost in Time: A New Temporal Benchmark for VideoLLMs

D. Cores*, M. Dorkenwald*, M. Mucientes, C. Snoek, Y. Asano

We introduce TVBench, a 10-task benchmark for temporal understanding in video–language models. It addresses shortcuts in existing benchmarks that let models answer from text or a single frame.

Paper Evaluation code Dataset
Efficient visual computationNeurIPS 2025Equal contribution

Elastic ViTs from Pretrained Models without Retraining

W. Simoncini*, M. Dorkenwald*, T. Blankevoort, C. Snoek, Y. Asano

We introduce SnapViT, a structured pruning method that adapts pretrained vision transformers to different compute budgets without retraining or labeled data.

Evaluated on DINO, SigLIPv2, DeiT, and AugReg models.

Paper Code Inference example Project
Representation learningECCV 2024Equal contribution

SIGMA: Sinkhorn-Guided Masked Video Modeling

M. Salehi*, M. Dorkenwald*, F. Thoker*, E. Gavves, C. Snoek, Y. Asano

We jointly learn a video encoder and its prediction targets through masked modeling. Balanced clustering prevents representation collapse and provides targets that capture semantic and temporal information.
